What a flake floor is

Installers also call this a chip or broadcast system, and it goes down as a stack of separate layers. After the slab is ground and repaired, a pigmented base coat is applied. While that base is still wet, colored vinyl flakes are thrown by hand across the surface. The flakes stick into the base and, in a full broadcast, cover it completely. Once everything has set, the loose excess is scraped and vacuumed away, and one or two clear topcoats seal the whole floor.

The finished surface has a terrazzo-like speckle and a very slight texture where the flakes overlap. That texture is part of the appeal: it gives tires and shoes something to grip and keeps the floor from looking like a sheet of glass.

Flake systems are the most common choice for garages in the Mohave Valley for good reason. They are durable, forgiving and good-looking without being fussy, and they fit almost any home.

Why flake works so well in a dusty valley

If you live near the river, you already know how quickly a fine layer of sand and dust settles on everything. On a solid-color floor, that dust is visible within a day. On a flake floor, it disappears into the pattern. The same is true of the water spots a boat trailer leaves behind, the scuff of a work boot and the outline of a tire that just came off a dirt road.

That camouflage means a flake floor looks clean longer between washes. You still need to sweep — grit is abrasive — but you do not have to do it every time a door opens.

  • Multi-tone blends mask dust, sand and small stains
  • Slight texture improves footing when wet
  • Thick, layered build resists impacts and hot tires
  • Repairs blend in more easily than on solid colors

Choosing a blend

Flake blends are mixes of several colors and flake sizes. The overall impression from standing height is quite different from what you see up close, so choosing from a small chip in your hand can be misleading. We bring large sample boards to every estimate and look at them in the actual space, with the garage door open and closed.

In this part of Arizona, a few families of color tend to look right and wear well:

Desert sand

Tans, creams and a touch of brown and black. Warm, light and very good at hiding the color of local dust.

River stone

Grays, soft blues and whites with a few dark accents, like the gravel bars along the water.

Slate

Charcoal and mid grays with white highlights. A cleaner, more modern look that still hides tire marks.

Custom blends

Pick colors to match cabinets, a truck, a team or the stucco of the house. Larger flake sizes give a bolder look.

Full broadcast or partial?

Most garages and workshops get a full broadcast. Partial broadcasts are popular in laundry rooms, casitas and commercial spaces where the base color is part of the design.

Full broadcast

  • Flakes cover the base completely
  • Maximum dust and stain camouflage
  • Thicker, tougher overall build
  • Most common for garages and shops

Partial broadcast

  • Flakes scattered over a visible base color
  • Lighter, more decorative look
  • Good for interiors and showrooms
  • Shows dust more than full coverage

How we install flake floors

  1. 1

    Grind and repair

    The slab is diamond-ground and vacuumed; cracks, spalls and pits are filled and ground smooth.

  2. 2

    Base coat

    A base coat tinted to complement the flake blend is spread evenly. Color matters even under full coverage because it shows between overlapping chips.

  3. 3

    Broadcast

    Flakes are thrown upward and allowed to fall, building even coverage across the whole floor without clumps or bare spots.

  4. 4

    Scrape and vacuum

    Once the base has set, loose and standing flakes are scraped down and vacuumed so the surface is ready for the clear coat.

  5. 5

    Clear topcoat

    One or two coats of clear, UV-stable polyaspartic seal the flakes. The number of coats controls how smooth the final texture feels.

Texture: smoother or grippier

The feel of a flake floor depends on the topcoat. A single clear coat over a full broadcast leaves a noticeable texture — good for grip, a bit harder to mop. A second coat fills more of the gaps between flakes and produces a smoother surface that is easier to clean but still has some tooth.

For garages where a boat is washed or where people walk in with wet feet from the pool, we often stay with a single top or add an anti-slip aggregate. For workshops where you roll on a creeper or wheel heavy tool chests, a smoother finish may be better. We will talk through how you use the space and adjust accordingly.

Flake outdoors and under carports

Flake systems also work on covered patios, porches and carport slabs. Outdoors, the topcoat must be fully UV-stable, and colors on the lighter end of the range stay noticeably cooler in the sun. Control joints should be honored so the coating can move with the concrete through the summer-to-winter swings.

For uncovered pool decks and walkways in full sun, we usually recommend a dedicated outdoor system instead. Our patio and concrete coatings page explains the options.

Living with a flake floor

Sweep or blow out the floor regularly, especially in the lanes where vehicles drive in. Mop or hose it off with a mild cleaner when it needs more. Clean oil and chemical spills promptly. Protect the surface under trailer jacks and motorcycle kickstands with small pads.

Over the years, the topcoat in high-traffic paths will lose some of its sheen. A maintenance recoat — a light sanding and a fresh clear coat — brings it back without touching the flake layer.

Where flake might not be the best choice

Flake is versatile, but it is not perfect for every situation. In spaces where you want a sleek, reflective look — a showroom or a modern living room — a solid or metallic finish may suit the design better. In industrial areas with heavy forklift traffic, a dense solid system with a thicker build is usually more appropriate. And in areas that get constant standing water, the slight texture of flake can hold puddles longer than a smooth sloped surface. We will recommend what fits rather than defaulting to the same floor every time.

Flake sizes and what they change

Vinyl flakes come in several sizes, from small chips about the size of a grain of rice to larger pieces closer to a fingernail. Smaller flakes create a tighter, finer texture that reads almost like granite from a distance. Larger flakes give a bolder, more obvious pattern. Most residential garages use a medium size, which balances visual interest with good coverage.

Size also affects texture. A full broadcast of large flakes can feel a little more pronounced underfoot until the topcoat fills it in. If you plan to roll on a creeper or mop often, mention it during the estimate and we will choose accordingly.

Matching flake to the rest of the property

Flake floors look best when they relate to the home around them. A warm stucco exterior with tan trim pairs naturally with sandy blends. A modern home with gray tones suits slate or river-stone mixes. Garage cabinets, the color of the door and even the vehicles inside can guide the choice. We have seen many clients pick a bold sample in hand and then choose something more neutral once they see it laid against the walls.

Decorative Flake Flooring: common questions

How long does a flake floor last?

A properly prepared and installed flake system in a residential garage should provide many years of service. Topcoat wear in traffic lanes is normal and can be refreshed with a maintenance coat.

Do the flakes come loose?

No. Each chip is pressed into the wet base and then buried under the clear coat, so there is no exposed edge to catch. They do not flake off with normal use.

Can I see a full sample before choosing?

Yes. We bring large sample boards to the estimate so you can see the blend in your own space and lighting.

Will a flake floor be too rough to clean?

A second clear topcoat smooths the surface significantly. We balance grip and cleanability based on how you use the space.

Can flake be used indoors?

Yes, especially in laundry rooms, mudrooms and casitas. Partial broadcasts or finer flake sizes look more refined indoors.

Can you add a contrasting border or parking pad?

Yes. Borders, parking pads and simple zones in a second blend or solid color can be built into the floor during installation.
