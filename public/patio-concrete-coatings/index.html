The trouble with bare outdoor concrete here

Outdoor slabs in the Mohave Valley live a hard life. They sit in direct sun for most of the year, absorbing heat until they are uncomfortable or even painful to walk on. They are scoured by windblown sand, stained by irrigation overspray and leaves, and occasionally hit by a monsoon downpour that dumps more water than the surface can shed.

Over time, bare concrete fades, develops hairline cracks, and loses its top layer to wear. Pool decks get slick where algae or mineral deposits form. Walkways chip at their edges. Painted patios peel within a season or two. A purpose-built outdoor coating system protects the slab and makes it more pleasant to use.

What we coat outdoors

Covered patios

Shaded entertaining areas where a flake or solid finish adds color and makes cleanup easy.

Pool decks

Textured, lighter coatings that give wet feet grip and reduce surface heat around the water.

Walkways and entries

Front walks and steps that make a first impression and take daily foot traffic.

Carports and RV pads

Slabs that hold vehicles under a roof but stay open to sun, wind and dust on the sides.

Porches and balconies

Smaller concrete surfaces near doors where water, dirt and footwear collect.

Shade structures

Slabs under ramadas and pergolas that are partly shaded and partly exposed through the day.

Keeping surfaces cooler

Color has a big effect on surface temperature. Dark concrete and dark coatings absorb more sunlight and heat up far more than light ones. On a summer afternoon, the difference between a charcoal surface and a light sand surface is easy to feel with bare feet.

For uncovered areas, especially around pools, we steer clients toward lighter tones: creams, light tans, pale grays and blends of those colors. Some outdoor systems also use materials designed to reflect more heat. No coating can make concrete cool to the touch at 115 degrees, but choosing the right color and texture makes a real, noticeable difference.

Grip around the pool and on wet surfaces

Safety comes first on any surface that gets wet. Smooth, glossy coatings are not appropriate for pool decks, walkways or steps. Outdoor systems are textured, either through the way the material is applied — such as a knockdown or broom-style texture — or through aggregates added to the coating. The goal is a surface that grips bare feet and shoes when wet without being so rough it scrapes knees or is hard to clean.

We discuss the level of texture with every client and can show samples. Families with young children often prefer a slightly more aggressive grip; homeowners who mostly entertain adults may want something a little smoother.

Outdoor system options

Some properties need both: a flake finish on the covered patio and a textured system on the open pool deck, with a clean transition between them.

Covered areas

  • Flake or solid color systems
  • UV-stable polyaspartic topcoat
  • Smoother, easier to clean
  • Best for patios, porches and carports

Fully exposed areas

  • Textured, breathable outdoor coatings
  • Lighter colors to manage heat
  • Built-in slip resistance
  • Best for pool decks and open walkways

Repairs come first

Most outdoor slabs need some repair before they are coated. Cracks are cleaned out and filled with flexible material that can move as the slab expands and contracts. Spalled or chipped areas, often at edges and steps, are rebuilt with repair mortar. Low spots that hold water may be addressed with a skim coat.

Control joints — the straight lines cut into a slab to guide where cracks form — are usually left open or filled with a flexible sealant. Covering them with a rigid coating would only invite a crack to appear in the new surface. We discuss joint treatment with you before the work starts.

Our outdoor process

  1. 1

    Assess

    We check the slab for cracks, drainage, old coatings and sealers, and review sun exposure for each area.

  2. 2

    The surface is pressure washed and mechanically prepared — ground or profiled — to remove weak concrete and old coatings.

  3. 3

  6. 6

    Foot traffic usually resumes within a day; furniture and vehicles return after the specified cure time.

Weather and timing

Outdoor coating work depends on the weather. In summer, slab surfaces in full sun can get too hot for good application by late morning, so we begin early and may coat shaded and sunny areas at different times of day. During monsoon season, we watch forecasts closely and protect fresh work from rain and blowing dust. Spring and fall offer the widest window and are popular times for patio and pool deck projects.

Care for coated outdoor concrete

Sweep or blow off dust and leaves regularly. Rinse with a hose, and scrub with a mild cleaner if needed. Avoid harsh chemicals and very high pressure washing directly at the surface. Keep pool chemicals off the coating when you can, and rinse splashes.

Outdoor coatings may need a refresh coat after years in the sun. When that time comes, the surface is cleaned, lightly prepared and resealed — much easier than starting from scratch. For covered garage-adjacent slabs, our decorative flake and polyaspartic pages have more detail.

Carports, RV pads and boat parking

Many properties in Fort Mohave and the surrounding area include a carport, an RV pad or a slab beside the house for boat parking. These surfaces carry heavy loads, sit partly or fully in the sun, and collect oil drips, trailer marks and windblown sand. Coating them makes cleanup easier and keeps the concrete from deteriorating.

For covered carports, a flake system with a UV-stable topcoat works well and matches a garage floor if you want a consistent look. For open pads in full sun, we usually recommend a breathable outdoor system with texture and a lighter color. Jack pads or plates under trailer jacks and stabilizers spread heavy point loads and protect any coating.

Matching outdoor spaces to the home

Outdoor coatings can tie a property together. A front walk, entry and covered patio finished in related colors look intentional, and colors chosen to complement the stucco, trim and roof make the house feel finished. We bring outdoor samples and look at them in place, in sun and shade, so you can see how they read from the street and from inside.

If you live in a community with design guidelines, visible surfaces such as front walkways may need approval. We can provide product information and physical samples for that process.

Front entries and curb appeal

The walk from the driveway to the front door is the first thing visitors see, and in many neighborhoods here it has faded, cracked or picked up stains from irrigation and leaves. Coating the entry walk, porch and steps refreshes the whole front of the house. Steps get extra attention: edges are repaired, the texture is chosen for grip and contrasting nosing can make each step easier to see.

Lighter, neutral colors suit most homes, and a matching finish on the covered porch ties the entry together.

Coating versus sealing

Not every outdoor slab needs a full coating. A penetrating sealer soaks into concrete, reduces staining and water absorption and leaves the natural look intact, though it does not hide cracks or change color. A coating system builds a new surface with color, texture and repairs underneath. During the estimate we will tell you which approach suits each surface and budget.

Patio & Concrete Coatings: common questions

Will a coating make my pool deck cooler?

Lighter colors and certain outdoor systems reduce surface heat noticeably compared with dark or bare concrete. No coating makes sun-baked concrete cold, but the difference is real.

Is the surface slippery around the pool?

Not when installed correctly. Outdoor systems are textured or include aggregate for grip on wet feet.

Can you coat over my painted patio?

Old paint and sealers must be removed first. Coating over them would make the new surface only as strong as the failing layer underneath.

What about cracks?

Cracks are repaired with flexible materials before coating. Slabs continue to move, so some cracks may reappear as fine lines over time.

Do you coat driveways?

Driveways see heavy loads and full sun. Depending on condition, we may recommend a penetrating sealer rather than a film coating. We will advise after seeing it.

How long before we can use the patio?

Most people can walk on it the next day. Furniture, grills and vehicles return after the cure time we give you, which depends on the system and weather.
