Concrete coatings in Fort Mohave

Life here is lived on concrete. It might as well be good concrete.

Fort Mohave sits low in the Mohave Valley, close to the Colorado River and a long way from mild weather. Summer afternoons push well past 110 degrees, the wind carries fine sand into every corner, and a typical garage here does far more than hold a car. It stores the boat, charges the side-by-side, keeps the fishing gear and coolers, and often doubles as a workshop or gym.

Bare concrete does not handle any of that gracefully. It soaks up oil and sunscreen, sheds dust that ends up on everything, and stains the first time a trailer drips lake water across it. We install coating systems that seal the slab, clean up with a hose or a mop, and stay bonded through the temperature swings this valley is known for.

The work is the same whether the floor belongs to a home near the golf courses, a shop off Highway 95 or a storage building in Mohave Valley: grind the slab properly, repair what needs repairing, check for moisture, and choose a system that suits how the space is really used. From there, the finish is your call — from practical flake to a metallic floor that looks like moving water. Start with our garage floor coatings or compare every system below.

Why choose us

Built for one of the hottest corners of Arizona.

Coatings that perform fine in milder places can struggle here. Materials cure faster on a hot slab, standard epoxy yellows when the garage door spends the afternoon open, and dust settles on wet coats if the work is not managed. We plan every job around those conditions instead of hoping for the best.

That means earlier start times through the summer, products chosen for their working time at the slab temperature on the day, and topcoats that hold their color in direct sun. It also means honesty: if a floor would be better served by waiting for a cooler morning, we will tell you.

01

Prep you can see

Every slab is diamond-ground with dust extraction. Cracks are opened and filled, spalls are rebuilt, and old paint or sealer is removed so nothing weak sits under the new floor.

02

Heat-aware scheduling

We check slab temperature before mixing and adjust batch sizes, product speeds and start times. Hot afternoons are for planning, not for spreading coatings on sunbaked concrete.

03

UV-stable tops

Aliphatic polyaspartic and urethane topcoats resist the amber shift that sunlight causes in ordinary epoxy, so a light gray floor stays light gray.

04

Straight answers

Written quotes list every step and every coat. If a cheaper approach will do the job, we say so. If a floor has moisture or structural problems a coating will not fix, we tell you that too.

Garage floors

Garages, carports and toy barns.

Around here a garage is rarely just a place to park. Many hold a boat on its trailer, a pair of personal watercraft, a side-by-side or a travel trailer between trips. Others are workshops with a welder in the corner, or air-conditioned man caves with a television and a fridge. Each of those uses puts different demands on a floor.

Trailer jacks and stabilizer pads press hard on a tiny area. Lake water, sunscreen and fuel drips find their way to the slab. Sand comes in on every tire and every pair of sandals. We build garage systems with enough thickness to resist point loads, a texture that stays safe when wet and a topcoat that cleans up with a quick rinse.

Residential flooring

Inside the house, too.

Seamless coatings make a lot of sense indoors in a dusty climate. With no grout lines, no carpet fibers and no seams for sand to settle into, a coated floor sweeps clean in a few minutes. It also feels cool underfoot during the long summer, which is part of why polished and coated concrete has become so popular in desert homes.

We coat living areas, kitchens, laundry rooms, casitas, home gyms and garages that have been converted into living space. Interior systems use low-odor materials and are finished in satin or gloss. If you want a floor that becomes the centerpiece of the room, a metallic finish brings in the blues, sands and grays of the landscape outside.

Before any interior work we test the slab for moisture, because a slab that pushes vapor will eventually push a coating off. If readings are high, we use a moisture-tolerant primer or recommend a different approach. Learn more about residential epoxy flooring and metallic floors.

Patios & pool decks

Outdoor concrete that you can actually walk on in August.

Dark or bare concrete in full sun gets hot enough to hurt. Lighter coatings reflect more of that sunlight and stay noticeably more comfortable, and a textured finish keeps wet feet from slipping around the pool. We coat patios, pool decks, entry walks, covered porches and the slabs under carports and shade structures.

Outdoor surfaces in this valley take a beating from more than heat. Monsoon storms drop heavy rain in a short time, wind drives sand across every surface, and irrigation overspray leaves mineral spots. We use UV-stable, breathable systems, repair cracks first and leave control joints able to move. Read more on our patio and concrete coatings page.

Local property requirements

What we look for on Fort Mohave properties.

Every property along the river corridor is a little different, but a few conditions come up again and again. Knowing them ahead of time keeps a project on schedule and out of trouble later.

Sealed or painted slabs
Many garages here were sealed by a builder or rolled with garage paint years ago. Both block adhesion and must be ground off completely before any new system goes down.
Moisture from below
Irrigated landscaping, older slabs without vapor barriers and areas near the river can push moisture up through concrete. We test before quoting and adjust the primer if needed.
Big temperature swings
Slabs expand in summer heat and contract on cold winter nights. Flexible base coats and honored control joints keep the coating from cracking along with the concrete.
Detached shops & outbuildings
Many lots include a separate shop, RV garage or storage building. These slabs are often thinner or less finished than the house, so they get a closer look during the estimate.
Community guidelines
Golf-course and planned neighborhoods may have rules for visible exterior surfaces. We provide samples and product sheets for any approval you need.
Seasonal residents
If you spend part of the year away, we can schedule work around your plans and coordinate access with a neighbor or property manager.

FAQ

Questions we hear most.

How hot is too hot to coat a garage floor here?

Slab temperature matters more than air temperature. In June through September we start at first light, check the concrete with an infrared thermometer and switch to slower-reacting products or smaller mixes as the slab warms. If a slab facing west is already too hot by mid-morning, we finish that side another day rather than rush it.

Will the coating peel where my tires sit?

Hot-tire lifting is almost always a preparation problem. Thin coatings rolled onto smooth or sealed concrete soften under a hot tire and release. We diamond-grind every slab so the base coat soaks into open concrete, and we build the system thick enough to handle tires coming off a long drive on Highway 95 in July.

Can you coat a garage that holds a boat or travel trailer?

Yes. Boat and toy garages are common in this area. Trailer jacks and stabilizer pads concentrate a lot of weight on a small point, so we suggest a heavier build and a set of jack pads or plates. Wet hulls and lake water are no problem for a properly sealed floor.

How long until we can use the floor again?

With a polyaspartic topcoat, most garages are ready for foot traffic the same evening and vehicles in a few days, depending on temperature. Full-epoxy and metallic interior systems need longer between coats, usually two to four days in total.

Do you coat carports and slabs that are not enclosed?

We do. Open slabs see full sun, blowing dust and occasional monsoon rain, so we use UV-stable materials, lighter colors where possible and a grip texture. Carport floors under a roof but open on the sides do very well with a flake and polyaspartic system.

Is the floor slippery when it gets wet?

Any smooth, glossy surface can be slick when wet. Chip floors already have some tooth, and a fine grit mixed into the last coat adds more where you need it. For patios and pool decks we always build in grip.

What does an estimate include?

We look at the slab, measure, check moisture and existing cracks, talk through how the space is used and show color samples. You get a written quote that lists preparation, repairs, each coat in the system and the expected timeline.

Do I need HOA approval?

Inside a garage or home, usually not. For visible exterior work such as front walkways or driveways, some communities want to approve colors. We are happy to provide product sheets and samples for your submission.
