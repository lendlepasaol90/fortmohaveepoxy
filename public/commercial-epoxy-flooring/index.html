What commercial floors need

A commercial floor has to do several jobs at once. It has to look clean and professional to customers. It has to stand up to whatever the business throws at it, whether that is rolling carts, dropped tools, salon chemicals or kitchen grease. It has to be safe to walk on, often when wet. And it has to be easy enough to clean that staff actually keep up with it.

Coated concrete checks all of those boxes in a way few other floors can. The surface is seamless and non-porous, so spills stay on top and nothing grows in grout lines. It resists a wide range of chemicals. It can be textured for traction. And it can be colored and finished to match a brand or design.

Working around your schedule

For most businesses, the biggest worry about a new floor is downtime. Closing for a week is not an option for a busy shop or a clinic with booked appointments. We plan commercial projects to minimize disruption.

Many jobs can be done over a weekend or across a few nights. Larger spaces can be phased, coating one area while the rest of the business keeps operating, then moving on. Fast-cure polyaspartic systems allow some areas to reopen within hours. We coordinate start times, access, ventilation and return-to-service times with you and, where needed, your landlord or property manager.

Safety and sanitation details

Commercial floors often need features that a home garage does not. Slip resistance is one. Aggregates can be built into the topcoat at different levels, from a light texture for retail floors to an aggressive grip in a commercial kitchen.

Integral cove base is another. In food service and medical spaces, the coating can curve up the wall several inches, creating a seamless, washable joint between floor and wall where dirt and moisture cannot collect. Drains, thresholds and transitions to other flooring are detailed so water goes where it should and edges do not chip.

  • Anti-slip aggregate options for wet and greasy areas
  • Integral cove base for sanitary floor-to-wall transitions
  • Chemical-resistant topcoats for salons, labs and shops
  • Line striping and zone marking where needed

Choosing a system

Commercial systems range from simple to very heavy-duty. Some typical pairings:

Light to moderate use

  • Offices, salons, retail
  • Solid, partial flake or metallic
  • Satin urethane or polyaspartic top
  • Focus on appearance and cleanability

Heavy use

  • Auto shops, kitchens, service bays
  • High-build epoxy or polyurea base
  • Full flake or aggregate texture
  • Focus on chemical and impact resistance

Shops in a hot climate

Service businesses along Highway 95 often work with roll-up doors open, which means hot air, blowing dust and direct sun on the first part of the floor. Vehicles come in with hot tires and drip fluids. Detailers use water and chemicals all day.

For these spaces we favor chemical-resistant, UV-stable systems with a flake or aggregate texture. A light-colored floor also brightens a shop considerably, making it easier to find dropped parts and see leaks. Solid color zones and striping can mark work bays, walkways and storage areas.

Maintenance for commercial floors

Most coated commercial floors can be cleaned with an auto scrubber or a mop and a neutral cleaner. Kitchens and shops may use degreasers suitable for the coating. Walk-off mats at entries reduce the grit that wears the surface. We provide a simple care sheet for staff at handover.

In high-traffic areas, the topcoat will wear over time. Planned maintenance recoats every few years keep the floor protected without a full replacement. For larger storage and industrial facilities, see warehouse floor coatings. For decorative showroom finishes, see metallic epoxy flooring.

Planning around tenants, landlords and inspections

Many commercial spaces in the area are leased, which adds a few steps to any flooring project. Landlords may want to approve the system, the schedule or the contractor's insurance before work begins. Neighboring tenants may share walls, ventilation or parking, and they deserve notice when grinding or coating is planned next door.

We help with that coordination. Product data and safety sheets, certificates of insurance and a written scope are available for property managers on request. For food service and medical businesses that face inspections, we discuss finish requirements such as cove base, drain details and cleanability at the start, so the floor helps rather than complicates the approval process.

Removing old commercial flooring

Commercial spaces often change hands, and each tenant leaves something behind: vinyl composition tile, sheet vinyl, carpet tile, ceramic tile or a worn-out coating. Some of these come up easily; others leave heavy adhesive that must be ground away before a new system can bond. Older buildings may also have flooring materials that need to be tested before they are disturbed, and we will flag anything that looks like it needs a professional assessment.

Once the slab is bare, we can see its true condition. Trenches from past plumbing changes, patched holes from removed fixtures and uneven areas are all common. We repair and level these so the finished floor is flat and continuous, and we explain anything that may still show through translucent finishes.

Making a floor part of the brand

A floor is one of the largest visual surfaces in any business. Color, sheen and texture all say something to the people walking in. A bright, clean floor in a service shop tells customers the work is careful. A metallic or satin finish in a salon or boutique sets a mood before anyone sees a product. Logos, borders and color-coded zones can all be built into a coated floor and will not wear away like a mat or decal.

We bring samples, discuss how the floor will look under your lighting, and help you balance appearance with the practical needs of the space.

What a commercial estimate covers

Our commercial walkthroughs cover more than square footage. We look at how people and equipment move through the space, which areas see water or chemicals, where drains and floor transitions sit, and what condition the slab is in under the existing flooring. We ask about operating hours, deliveries and any upcoming inspections. That information shapes the system, the schedule and the phasing plan.

The written proposal that follows lays out each area, the system planned for it, the repairs included and the expected return-to-service time. If you need options at different budgets, we can price them side by side.

Commercial Epoxy Flooring: common questions

Can you work after hours?

Yes. Many commercial projects are scheduled overnight or on weekends so the business can stay open.

How long until we can reopen?

It depends on the system. Fast-cure options can allow foot traffic within hours; heavier systems may need a day or more. We plan this into the schedule.

Do you install cove base?

Yes. Integral cove base is available for kitchens, medical spaces and anywhere a seamless floor-to-wall joint is needed.

Can the floor match our brand colors?

Solid color systems can be tinted to many colors, and flake blends can be customized. We will provide samples for approval.

Will odors affect neighboring businesses?

We use low-odor products where possible, ventilate during application and coordinate timing with property managers when spaces share air.

Can you coat around fixed equipment and racks?

Often, yes. We coat neatly around fixed items, and where equipment can be moved temporarily, coating underneath gives a cleaner, more sanitary result.
