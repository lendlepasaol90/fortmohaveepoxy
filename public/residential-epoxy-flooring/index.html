Why coated concrete makes sense indoors

Many homes in Fort Mohave and the surrounding valley sit on concrete slabs, and many homeowners eventually get tired of what covers them. Carpet traps the fine dust that drifts in every time a door opens. Tile looks good until the grout lines darken and crack. Wood and laminate do not love the dry heat or the occasional leak from a swamp cooler or water heater.

A coated floor removes those trade-offs. It is a single continuous surface without seams, so there is nowhere for sand to hide. It wipes clean with a damp mop. It does not care about low humidity. And because concrete holds onto the coolness of the ground beneath it, a coated slab stays comfortable on bare feet during the long hot season.

Interior coatings are also more flexible in appearance than most people expect. They can be a soft, almost matte stone gray that disappears into a modern room, a warm tan that echoes the desert outside, or a deep metallic that becomes the first thing visitors notice.

Rooms we coat

Living and great rooms

Large open areas where a continuous floor without transitions makes the space feel bigger and calmer.

Garage conversions

Finishing a converted garage slab so it reads as living space rather than a garage with furniture.

Moisture testing comes first

The biggest risk with any interior floor coating is moisture vapor coming up through the slab. Concrete is not waterproof. If there is no vapor barrier beneath it, or if irrigation, a high water table or a plumbing issue keeps the soil damp, water vapor will migrate upward. A coating seals the top of the slab, and if enough vapor collects underneath, it can push the coating into blisters or break the bond.

Older homes and slabs near irrigated landscaping deserve extra attention. Before we quote an interior floor, we take moisture readings in several spots. If the numbers are within the tolerance of a standard primer, we proceed normally. If they are higher, we use a moisture-mitigating primer designed to block vapor, and we explain the added cost and reason clearly in the quote.

In the rare case that moisture levels are beyond what any coating can manage, we will tell you that too. A floor that fails in a year helps no one.

Removing old flooring

Most interior projects start with something on the slab: carpet and pad, vinyl plank, tile or the adhesive left behind by any of them. Carpet comes up quickly. Tile and thinset take more effort, and mastic adhesives have to be removed fully because a coating will not bond to glue.

We can handle demolition as part of the job, or coordinate with you if you prefer to remove the old flooring yourself. Either way, the slab is diamond-ground afterward to remove adhesive residue and create a uniform surface. This is also when we find out what the concrete really looks like: patches, old plumbing trenches, cracks and color variations all show up once the floor is bare.

Some of those features can be repaired invisibly beneath a solid or metallic finish. Others, like a long plumbing trench, may show as a subtle line under certain translucent finishes. We point these out before work begins so the final look is not a surprise.

Finish choices for living spaces

Interior floors give you more design range than garages. The most popular directions are:

  • Solid satin — a single soft color with a low-sheen top. Quiet, modern and very forgiving of footprints and dust.
  • Light flake — a fine, partial chip broadcast that adds a little texture and visual interest without looking like a garage.
  • Metallic — hand-worked pearl pigments that create depth and movement. See our metallic epoxy flooring page.
  • Gloss solid — a reflective surface that brightens rooms with limited light, best in spaces with lower traffic.

How an interior job runs

  1. 1

    Plan and protect

    We agree on the layout, sequence and access. Walls, cabinets, door frames and adjacent rooms are masked and protected.

  2. 2

    Remove and grind

    Old flooring and adhesive are removed. The slab is ground with dust extraction and vacuumed thoroughly.

  3. 3

    Repair and detail

    Cracks, holes and old trenches are filled. Thresholds and transitions to other flooring are planned so edges finish neatly.

  4. 4

    Prime and build

    A primer — moisture-mitigating if needed — goes on first, followed by the body coat or decorative layer.

  5. 5

    Topcoat

    A clear, low-odor topcoat sets the final sheen. We can add a fine texture in kitchens, baths or entries.

  6. 6

    Return to use

    Light foot traffic is usually possible after a day; furniture can return after the time we specify for your system.

Living in the house during the work

Many clients stay home while we work. Grinding is loud but fast, and our extractors capture most of the dust. The coating stages are quiet. We use low-odor, low-VOC products for interior work, but there is still some smell during application, and we ventilate the area as we go. If anyone in the household is sensitive to odors, staying elsewhere for a night may be more comfortable.

Rooms being coated are off-limits until the coating has cured enough for foot traffic. For homes where the coated area is the main path through the house, we plan the sequence so you always have a way in and out. Pets should be kept away from wet coatings — paw prints are hard to repair.

Comfort and care

Coated floors are firm, just like tile or polished concrete. Area rugs soften spaces where you stand for a long time, such as in front of a kitchen counter, and they add warmth to living areas in winter. Use felt pads on chair and table legs and avoid dragging heavy furniture.

Day-to-day care is a dust mop and an occasional damp mop with a neutral cleaner. Avoid harsh chemicals, vinegar-heavy solutions and oil soaps, which can leave a film. With a little care, the topcoat keeps its sheen for many years, and it can be refreshed with a maintenance coat when needed.

Converted garages and casitas

Garage conversions are common as families add a bedroom, office or game room. The challenge is that garage slabs often slope toward the door and may have oil stains, expansion joints in odd places and a rougher finish than an interior slab. We grind the surface, fill joints if you want them hidden, and can apply a self-leveling underlayment where the slope needs correcting before the coating goes down.

Detached casitas and guest houses often sit empty for part of the year. A coated floor is a good fit: it does not hold dust or odors, it is not damaged by the heat inside a closed building, and it needs only a quick mop before guests arrive.

Matching the floor to the rest of the house

Interior floors work best when they connect with the cabinets, walls and furniture around them. Warm desert homes with wood tones and earthy paint suit tan, greige and soft stone colors. Contemporary homes with white walls and black accents look sharp with cool grays or a silver metallic. Bringing samples into the rooms and looking at them morning and evening is the most reliable way to choose.

Residential Epoxy Flooring: common questions

Does an epoxy floor feel cold in winter?

It feels similar to tile. Most people appreciate the coolness in summer and use area rugs in living spaces during the cooler months.

Is there a strong smell?

We use low-odor, low-VOC interior products. There is some smell during application that fades quickly with ventilation, usually within a day.

Can you coat over tile?

We generally recommend removing tile. Coating over it risks the grout lines showing through and depends on the tile staying bonded for the life of the new floor.

Will it scratch?

Any floor can scratch. A satin finish hides minor scratches far better than gloss, and furniture pads and regular sweeping prevent most wear.

Can a coated floor go in a bathroom?

Yes, with a textured topcoat for traction and careful detailing around fixtures. Coated floors are waterproof on the surface and easy to keep clean.

What if the slab has moisture problems?

We test first. Elevated readings call for a moisture-mitigating primer, which we include in the quote with a clear explanation.
